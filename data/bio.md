
My name is Uriel Legaria. I am a first-year CAMD PhD student who studies how structure and curious dynamics emerge in complex systems. My prior work centered on modeling cancer and understanding malignant cell mobility through **Networks** and **Cellular automata**. At present, my work turns to a system at a completely distinct scale with its own challenges and intricacies: **Cities**.

These systems are distinct, yet share some similar structures. Questions that arise in tumor biology — how local interactions produce global patterns, how networks constrain and guide mobility, how a system reorganizes itself under pressure — reappear when considering the dynamics of cities and their policies. 

My current projects investigate how street-level characteristics and social dynamics shape the urban tapestry and the experiences of citizens. My aim is to make these tools accessible and actionable, so that they can inform urban planning in Mexico and contexts where data might not be readily available.

Concretely I combine **Street View Imagery**, **Computer Vision** and **Machine Learning** to:

1. Propose affordable methods to compute key urban form characteristics such as **Building Height** or **Facade Materials**, which inform housing policies, are used to estimate earthquake resilience, and to assess the climate impact of buildings.

2. Propose contextual systems that evaluate the presence of walkable infrastructure from images, considering cultural and place specific factors that influence **Walkability**.

3. Study how built environment characteristics and building materials relate to Environmental **Sensor Data** such as temperature and PM-2.5.
